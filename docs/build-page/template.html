Fridge Counter Build

All six pieces exist. The counter program runs on your bench board and reports to the live server, and the dashboard is public at hollygrovenola.org/fridge. Nothing has been printed yet, and the fridge unit waits for the Walter board.

Update 7 · Saturday, November 21, 2026. Final update for this session. The test unit reports over WiFi on its own power, with a real switch. Open decision: indoors or outdoors for the fridge unit.

How the whole thing works

A magnet on the fridge door and a switch on the frame tell a small computer when the door opens. The computer sends each opening over the cell network to the Hollygrove server, which adds them up and shows hourly totals on the public page. Pick a part to zoom in on it.

The six deliverables

  • 1. Counter program for the ESP32

    Running on your board

    The board on your desk runs the program as test device bench-01. Over HTTPS to the live server it sent heartbeats and simulated door openings, all accepted, and it kept reporting for hours.

    • Each opening is recorded with its time and how long the door stayed open. Events are kept through power cuts and outages and resent.
    • WiFi today. The Walter LTE version compiles, but it has never run on a Walter, and it does not yet switch on the Walter's sensor power rail.
    • A real reed switch is now wired to it and counting. A WiFi fault with the eero access points was fixed by lowering the board's transmit power.
    • Not tested: real sensors, the status light's colours.
    src\, docs\firmware.md

  • 2. Wiring diagram

    Done

    One pin layout works for all your board types. Each part is tagged as in use, on hand, or to buy.

    docs\wiring\

  • 3. Server and database

    Live

    A seventh container on the hollygrove VM, beside the website and NextCloud, with its own small database. Public numbers are hourly totals, about a minute behind, never the time of one opening. Bench data stays out of the public numbers.

    • Backups: TrueNAS now snapshots the whole VM daily and keeps 14 days; the VM copies the fridge database nightly. Both are on the same storage pool as the VM.
    server\; on the VM /opt/hollygrove/fridge/

  • 4. Dashboard

    Live, sample data

    hollygrovenola.org/fridge is a short page with two cards: sample data (made-up numbers) and live data from the WiFi test unit at the house, labelled as a test. No navigation-bar link yet. The picture below is from before the hub was added.

  • 5. Demo door and frame

    Rev 2, not printed

    Built around the white surface-mount switch you measured. 152.4 mm tall with a 50.8 mm deep entry. Door and frame print together with the hinge already joined. The switch sticks inside the doorway and the magnet on the back of the door, 3 mm apart when shut, held by their foam tape.

    • A separate base plate, held by two zip ties, keeps it from tipping; it needs rubber feet so it does not slide.
    • Checked by calculation only. The magnet pocket prints as a 15 mm bridge and may sag a little.
    cad\demo-door\print\ holds the two files to print · hinge by Nick Johnson, CC BY-SA

  • 6. Case, power and weatherproofing

    Designed, not printed

    A printable bench case for each of your dev boards, and carriers that drop the electronics into a store-bought weatherproof box. There is now a carrier for the Walter: it plugs into a small prototyping board with screw terminals, and the carrier holds that board and the LTE antenna.

    • Power, first install: a USB adapter on the inverter's 120 V output. Your LM2596 converters must not go on the 48 V battery.
    • Several dimensions come from documents, not a ruler; the Walter ones are from its datasheet. The case README has a caliper checklist.
    cad\enclosure\print\, docs\power-and-enclosure.md
